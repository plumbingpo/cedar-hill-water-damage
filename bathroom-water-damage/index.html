Bathroom Water Damage: What Cedar Hill Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. We help Cedar Hill homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

What It Looks Like

  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
